# Binding the future self

Ulysses didn't plan to resist the Sirens. He had himself tied to the mast while
he was still thinking clearly. **Present bias** means the tempted self will
value the distraction more than the planner does, and argument won't change that
in the moment. What the planner **can** change in advance is which options that
self will face and what they cost. Theory:
[akrasia-explained.md](../akrasia-plan/references/akrasia-explained.md).

## How to run it

First find the failure pattern (drifting? delay? low value?), then choose the
**lightest** device that fits. Heavy devices built for light problems breed
resentment and get abandoned.

## Device menu (lightest first)

| Device                   | Example                                                | Fits              |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------- |
| **Friction**             | log out, delete the app, phone in another room        | drifting          |
| **Easy default**         | file left open, bag packed, the first action pre-set   | slow starts       |
| **Social**               | tell someone the specific commitment and its date     | delay, low value  |
| **Co-working**           | a set session with a partner, in person or on video   | drifting, starts  |
| **Self-set deadlines**   | spaced milestones with a referee                      | delay             |
| **Stakes**               | money at risk on a commitment service                 | repeated breaking |
| **Hard block**           | a site blocker locked until a set time                | drifting          |

Each device in detail, and when each one backfires:
[commitment-device-menu.md](references/commitment-device-menu.md).

## Design rules

- **Specific and checkable.** "At least 500 words of section 2 sent to Sam by
  Thursday 5pm", not "work on the draft".
- **The referee isn't you.** A commitment only you can judge is a wish.
- **Spaced beats single.** Self-imposed deadlines help, but evenly spaced ones
  helped more than one final date (Ariely & Wertenbroch, 2002).
- **Plan the exception.** Decide in advance the one legitimate way out
  (illness, a real emergency), so a slip doesn't turn into abandoning the
  device. "Never miss twice": one miss is a blip, two is the start of a new
  pattern.
- **Keep stakes proportionate.** Stake what stings but doesn't frighten. The
  aim is keeping the commitment, not punishment.

## Output

```
Commitment:  <specific and checkable>
Deadline:    <date and time>
Referee:     <a person or tool, not me>
Device:      <from the menu>
Stake:       <what happens if missed>
Exception:   <the one pre-agreed way out>
Review on:   <date, usually the next akrasia-review>
```
