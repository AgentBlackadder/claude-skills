# Sources for the akrasia skill set

Every citation used across `akrasia-plan`, `akrasia-contrast`, `akrasia-focus`,
`akrasia-unstick`, `akrasia-commit` and `akrasia-review`, with how much weight
each can bear.

> **Provenance.** These references were written from memory on 2026-09-27. None
> of the papers or books was opened while writing them. Titles, years and
> findings are given to the best of that recall. Check any of them before
> quoting it anywhere public.

## Evidence grades

- **Strong**: a meta-analysis, or a finding replicated widely by independent
  groups.
- **Moderate**: several supporting studies, mostly from one research programme,
  or a single well-cited study with plausible mechanisms.
- **Weak / contested**: a single study, or a finding that failed replication.
- **Practitioner**: a useful method or heuristic with no formal evidence base.
  Use it, but don't cite it as research.

## Heuristics in this set that are not findings

Say these are heuristics whenever you use them:

- the ×1.5 estimate multiplier before a personal ratio exists
- planning to about 70% of available focus time
- the 70% hit-rate threshold for planning less
- three to four hours of real focus as a strong day
- session lengths (10, 25, 50, 90 minutes) beyond the Pomodoro convention

## Claims deliberately left out

- **"It takes N minutes to refocus after an interruption."** This popular figure
  is poorly traced to its source. Only the direction (switching costs something)
  is used.
- **"21 days to form a habit."** No good basis; see Lally et al. above.
- **Ultradian 90-minute focus cycles as fact.** The evidence for applying these
  to knowledge work is weak. The 90-minute block is offered as a format, not a
  biological law.
- **Ego depletion.** Not used as a mechanism; see Hagger et al. above.
